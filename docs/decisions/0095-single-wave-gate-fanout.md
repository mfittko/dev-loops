# 0095. Single-wave gate fan-out without a primer barrier

## Status

Amends [0056](0056-bounded-parallel-gate-dispatch.md).
Amends [0083](0083-raise-claude-fanout-concurrency-cap-to-4.md).

It amends 0086's decision that "the primer stays mandatory for ordering", 0048's `maxAnglesPerGroup` default of 3 and `maxConcurrent` default of 4, 0056's repo value `maxConcurrent: 3` aligned with `queue.maxParallel`, 0083's Claude cap of 4, and 0047's "never dynamic packing" wording. It does not amend 0039: 0047 already scoped 0039's one-reviewer rule to one reviewer per dispatch unit, and a packed unit is one dispatch unit. It supersedes the mandatory-primer contract-rule assumption of [issue #1462](https://github.com/mfittko/dev-loops/issues/1462) and [issue #1468](https://github.com/mfittko/dev-loops/issues/1468) by reference. The rest of those records stays unchanged.

## Context

`GATE-EXEC-PRIME` required every gate fan-out to build the shared prefix, dispatch a lead reviewer or a dedicated primer, wait for an observable barrier, and only then release the remaining reviewers. On completion-only harnesses the default lead-reviewer form serialized one full review before the rest of the wave. A dedicated primer avoided that wait but added one model spawn. Issues #1462 and #1468 justified the barrier when the shared provider-visible prefix carried the expensive common corpus.

ADR 0086 moved the bulk evidence behind `requiredReads`. Reviewer prompts became bounded work orders of at most 30 KB, and each reviewer loads the large evidence after spawn. A primer can now warm only the compact shared request prefix. It cannot establish reuse for the post-spawn tool reads that dominate evidence loading. ADR 0086 already recorded the primer as non-load-bearing.

A typical round also needed more than one wave. Measured with `resolveFanoutGroups` and the emitter cap-split under this repo's `.devloops` and the shipped group table, a draft round of 10 to 12 angles emitted 5 units, a preApproval round of 10 angles 6 units, and one of 12 angles 7 units. With `maxConcurrent: 3` that is two or three waves. Larger unit bounds alone do not close the gap: configured groups are never merged, so the shipped 22-angle draft catalog still resolves to 7 base units and this repo's 20-angle draft pool to 6, and a partial round with one fresh angle in each of the 4 draft configured groups plus leftovers resolves to 5 or more.

## Decision

The primer barrier is removed. After the round context is built and validated and the emitter writes the bounded work orders, the gate coordinator releases the first reviewer wave immediately, bounded only by `resolveFanoutEffectiveConcurrency` (`GATE-EXEC-FIRST-WAVE-RELEASE`). No primer spawn or lead reviewer precedes that wave. `GATE-EXEC-PRIME` and `GATE-EXEC-PRIMER-EVIDENCE` are retired, and the primer-only evidence module and the fan-in primer flags are removed.

Cache priming is optional and non-semantic. A harness or execution adapter may prime only when its capability and measured economics justify it. Priming never changes review semantics or evidence, never reduces reviewer independence, and never becomes a fan-in or verdict precondition. Opaque or unavailable cache telemetry records `cacheReuseVerified: false`: it is never described as verified reuse, and missing telemetry never fails the gate. Recorded telemetry stays an optional measurement input under `GATE-EXEC-CACHE-TELEMETRY`.

Every planned round is one wave. `REVIEWER_UNIT_MAX_ANGLES` and `REVIEWER_UNIT_BUDGET.maxAngles` are 5. The `gates.fanout.maxAnglesPerGroup` default is 5. The shipped `holistic` singleton group is removed: `holistic` stays a mandatory angle with its own prompt, findings artifact and provenance entry, joins the leftover auto-chunk pool, and may share a reviewer unit with other leftover angles. A repo restores the `holistic` singleton unit by adding `{ name: holistic, angles: [holistic] }` to its `gates.fanout.groups` override, with no code change. The `REVIEWER_UNIT_BUDGET` turn and tool-call limits (45 and 50) stay unchanged.

Packing (`GATE-EXEC-FANOUT-CAPACITY`): `resolveFanoutDispatch` in `write-gate-context.mjs` derives the base units, which are the pending units after the emitter's cap-split at 5 angles. When they exceed the effective `maxConcurrent`, it merges whole base units first-fit decreasing by angle count, ties broken by unit name, into at most effective `maxConcurrent` units of at most 5 angles. A merged unit's name and provenance `group` join its member unit names in sorted order. A base unit stays whole, and a round whose count fits is left unpacked, which keeps a restored `holistic` singleton alone. The packed list is recorded in the context artifact's `fanout.groups` and `pendingGroups`. When no packing fits, the writer refuses fail-closed before writing any artifact and names the fresh angle count, the capacity and the remedies (raise `maxConcurrent`, disable angles, or rely on dynamic pruning), following the `REVIEWER_WORK_ORDER_MAX_BYTES` refusal precedent. `gates.fanout.sequential: true` and `mode: per-angle` stay explicit opt-outs that neither pack nor refuse. The standalone `review` gate resolves a larger full-PR angle set (33 angles under shipped defaults) and keeps its unpacked multi-wave plan.

Pairing checks the dispatched membership. The request plan's `requestGroups` group by model and are never the membership source. `write-gate-findings-log.mjs` records each dispatch unit's name and angles from the keyed context artifact's `fanout.groups` as `provenance.dispatchUnits`. The write side and the read side (`detect-checkpoint-evidence.mjs`) accept a shared reviewer only inside one recorded unit, and accept that unit only when it is a union of whole base units with at most 5 angles. Base units come from `resolveFanoutGroups` over the ledger's fresh and carried angles in the gate's angle-pool order, never ledger order; `resolveFanoutDispatch` orders the dispatched angles the same way. They are cap-split at 5 and do not depend on the harness clamp, so the verdict does not change between harnesses. An empty recorded membership is rejected. A ledger without recorded membership falls back to the base units: an unpacked round still passes and a reviewer shared across a packed unit fails closed.

Packing merges configured groups, which 0047 excluded with "never dynamic packing" and its "bug-hunting angles kept least grouped" rationale. We accept that tradeoff: packing is deterministic and auditable from the recorded `fanout.groups`, it runs only when the round would otherwise take a second wave, and every angle keeps its own prompt, findings artifact and provenance entry. A repo that wants bug-hunting angles isolated restates its `gates.fanout.groups` table with smaller groups, lowers the round's angle count, or sets `mode: per-angle`.

Capacity is `maxConcurrent` x 5 angles. The `gates.fanout.maxConcurrent` default and this repo's value are 5, and the Claude cap (`CLAUDE_MAX_EFFECTIVE_CONCURRENT`) is 5, so capacity is 25 and a full 22-angle shipped draft round is one wave. This repo keeps `queue.maxParallel: 3`. The combined peak is 3 runners x 5 units = 15 concurrent reviewer units, plus one driver and one gate coordinator per runner. `GATE-EXEC-DISPATCH-RETRY-BACKOFF` absorbs transient 429s. If 429s rise, lower `queue.maxParallel` first and keep `maxConcurrent` at 5 so a single round stays one wave.

Cross-harness decision: the unit bound 5, the `maxAnglesPerGroup` default 5, the `maxConcurrent` default 5, the Claude cap 5, packing and the `holistic` singleton removal apply to every repo and harness. `sequential: false` stays unchanged. Pi keeps one `runs.all([...])` call per wave, so every round is one call. A consumer that overrides `gates.fanout.groups` wholesale keeps its own table. A consumer that set `maxAnglesPerGroup` above 3 was cap-split at 3 and is now cap-split at 5. A repo that pins `maxConcurrent` below 5 or `maxAnglesPerGroup` below 5 may now get a `GATE-EXEC-FANOUT-CAPACITY` refusal on full rounds; the remedies are to raise `maxConcurrent`, disable angles, or rely on dynamic pruning.

A non-zero count of `reviewer_budget_exhausted` blockers on 5-angle units stops the PR for an operator decision on `REVIEWER_UNIT_BUDGET`.

This record also corrects ADR 0086's stated reason for the unhashed `context` read. ADR 0086 says the `context` entry carries no hash "because the context JSON embeds the prefix identity". That reason is wrong: the context artifact embeds no prefix identity, and the prefix hash lives only in the request plan (`sharedPrefixHash`) and on sentinels. The actual reason: `write-gate-context.mjs` writes the context JSON last as the completion marker, and the JSON contains the `requiredReads` manifest itself and a per-write `loggedAt`, so the prefix cannot hash it. Consequence: the context JSON and its `.adjacentCode` are not tamper-bound.

We rejected keeping the barrier as an optional config flag, because a gate phase whose only effect is a possible cache write adds a model-mediated serialization step with no correctness value. We rejected raising the unit bound alone, because the `holistic` singleton still forced a fifth unit. We rejected raising `queue.maxParallel`, because the combined peak would grow without shortening a round. We rejected up to 6 angles per unit for full rounds, because it spreads the fixed turn and tool-call budget thinner, and two waves for full rounds, because the invariant would then not hold for the most expensive round. We rejected re-deriving packed units at read time, because the result would depend on the reader's harness clamp.

## Consequences

A typical 10-to-12-angle round emits at most 4 units and releases them in one wave, under both the Claude clamp and a Pi env. A full round is one wave too: the shipped 22-angle draft catalog packs its 7 base units into 5 units, and this repo's 20-angle draft pool packs its 6 base units into 4 units of 5 angles. Carry-forward, partial and single-angle rounds dispatch grouped with fewer units, also in one wave. A round above capacity, or one whose base units cannot be packed into effective `maxConcurrent` units, is refused instead of planned as a second wave. Reviewer breadth is unchanged: the resolved angle set, per-angle findings artifacts, fresh-context isolation, carry-forward proof and the gate verdict rules stay the same; only the number of angles per unit and the unit count change. The prefix-hash, required-read hash, sentinel, same-head rebuild and retirement, and fan-in provenance guards stay load-bearing. Each reviewer unit now spends the same 45-turn, 50-tool-call budget on up to 5 angles, so the transcript measurement tracks `reviewer_budget_exhausted` counts. `test/github/fanout-single-wave.test.mjs`, `test/github/fanout-pairing-write-read.test.mjs`, `packages/core/test/reviewer-unit-bound.test.mjs`, `packages/core/test/config.test.mjs` and `test/contracts/gate-reviewer-work-order-contract.test.mjs` pin the single-wave plan, the bound, the `holistic` placement and the contract wording.
